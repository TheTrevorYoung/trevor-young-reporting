About Trevor

Trevor Young Reporting is where I document the life I’m building and report what I learn from it.

I’ve spent years moving between Sierra Leone and the United States, business and public-interest work, technology and real-world operations. I’m interested in what happens between an idea and the result, especially the parts that are usually edited out.

TYR lets me follow those questions in public. Sometimes that means reporting a story. Sometimes it means using AI to solve an operating problem, building an organization, testing a health goal, traveling, dealing with a setback or trying to understand why a system works the way it does.

Participant-reporter

I’m not presenting myself as the expert on every subject I touch. My role is closer to a participant-reporter: enter the problem, ask better questions, gather evidence, try something when appropriate, make a decision and report what happened.

“The topics can change because the underlying story does not.”

One life, several worlds

Published reporting is an important part of TYR, and it now has its own formal portfolio. But it is one lane, not the entire platform. Projects, AI in Real Life, Building It, 30 Days, Life & Place, travel, health, family, Sierra Leone and the United States all belong here when they reveal something worth documenting.

The goal is not to force every part of life into journalism. The goal is to make the work visible enough that a reader can see what was tried, what was learned, what changed and what still does not make sense.

Published work

When a piece clears the editorial threshold, it is published with a permanent URL, byline, publication date and correction trail so it can stand independently as professional work.
